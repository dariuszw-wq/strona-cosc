Family reunification in Poland

Bringing a spouse or children to Poland is a documented procedure, and it usually fails on paperwork from abroad rather than on the substance of the case.

Home (EN) › Family

Who can be brought to Poland

A residence permit for family reunification covers, in principle, your spouse and your minor children. The rules differ depending on your own status: a family member of a Polish citizen, of an EU citizen, and of a third-country national holding a Polish residence permit are three different legal paths with different requirements.

What the office actually checks

  • Your own status — what permit you hold and how long you have held it.
  • Proof of the relationship — marriage and birth certificates, translated by a sworn translator (tłumacz przysięgły) and, depending on the issuing country, legalised or apostilled.
  • Income — stable and regular, sufficient for the whole family without social assistance.
  • Accommodation — a place of residence in Poland, documented.
  • Health insurance for each family member.

Marriages are examined for genuineness. Where the authority suspects a marriage of convenience it can call both spouses for separate interviews and refuse the permit. This is a normal part of the procedure, not an accusation, but it is worth being prepared for it.

Documents from abroad

Foreign civil-status documents are the most common reason these files stall. Each needs a sworn translation into Polish, and depending on the country of issue either an apostille or consular legalisation. Getting this wrong means a request to supply missing documents, with at least 7 days to comply (art. 64 § 2 k.p.a.) — and documents from abroad rarely arrive within 7 days.

Filing and fees

The application is filed by or for the family member, electronically through the MOS system. Stamp duty for a temporary residence permit is 340 PLN, plus 100 PLN for issuing the residence card.
