The most important rule

What your right to benefits depends on

It is not citizenship that decides your right to a benefit, but a combination of four elements. The same foreigner may be entitled to one benefit and not to another.

🪪

Residence title

The type and basis of your legal stay (stamp after filing the application, residence card, permanent residence). The "stronger" it is, the broader your access to benefits.

📄

Right to work

A work permit, a declaration, or temporary residence and work — plus the annotation "access to the labour market", which is crucial for 800+.

🏥

ZUS coverage

Whether and to which insurance you are registered — especially sickness insurance. Your allowances depend on it: sickness, maternity, care.

💰

Income criterion

Some family benefits and social assistance depend on income per person in the family. ZUS allowances — do not.

In short: the most reliable are ZUS benefits (they depend on contributions, not on citizenship). Family benefits and social assistance more often require a "stronger" residence title than just the stamp after filing the application.

ZUS

ZUS allowances — what legal work and contributions unlock

Social insurance benefits depend on being covered by insurance (especially sickness insurance), not on citizenship. If you work legally and are registered with ZUS, you have the same access to them as a Polish citizen.

Sickness allowance

After the so-called qualifying period: 30 days for compulsory insurance (employment contract) or 90 days for voluntary insurance (mandate contract).

Care allowance

For caring for a sick child or another family member. No qualifying period — from the first day of sickness insurance.

Maternity allowance

On being covered by sickness insurance and the birth/adoption of a child. Also no qualifying period.

Rehabilitation benefit

After the sickness allowance is exhausted, when further treatment offers a prospect of regaining the ability to work.

Accident allowance

When the inability to work results from an accident at work or an occupational disease — no qualifying period.

Pension and retirement

Long-term benefits, depending on the insurance history and statutory conditions.

With a mandate contract, always check: whether you were registered for sickness insurance. Without it there is no sickness or maternity allowance. It is worth asking the employer for confirmation of registration (ZUS ZUA/RCA).
Family

800+ and family benefits

The 800+ child-raising benefit is available, among others, to foreigners who hold a residence card with the annotation "access to the labour market", if they live in Poland together with their children — as well as to people with permanent residence, refugee status, subsidiary or humanitarian protection. Excluded are, among others, foreigners with the right to work for up to 6 months, those staying for studies or on the basis of a visa alone.

The family allowance additionally depends on the income criterion. For a foreigner, the key factors are the appropriate residence title and the PESEL numbers of family members.

Labour office

Unemployment benefit

To register at the labour office as an unemployed person, a foreigner must hold an appropriate residence title (among others permanent residence, temporary residence for a family member of a Polish citizen, temporary residence and work, or a visa for the purpose of work). The right to the benefit arises after proving at least 365 days of work with Labour Fund contributions paid within the 18 months before registration.

In practice: after a few months of work the service period is usually not yet enough; after about a year the situation looks much better. Even without a right to the benefit, you can use some labour-market services. Full guide →
Social assistance

Social assistance and municipal benefits

A periodic or purpose-specific allowance or a housing supplement may be available, but only to foreigners from a narrow statutory catalogue — most often with permanent residence, EU long-term resident status, or temporary residence granted on specific grounds (e.g. as a family member of a Polish citizen). Temporary residence "and work" alone is usually not enough. An income criterion also applies.

This is where disputes most often arise at the intersection of the type of residence and the interpretation of the social assistance centre. Before filing, it is worth checking the exact residence basis. Full guide →
Note

Most common mistakes when assessing entitlements

1. Confusing residence with the right to benefits

Legal residence does not automatically mean the right to all allowances.

2. Overlooking the type of insurance

With a mandate contract you must separately check sickness insurance — part of the benefits depends on it.

3. No analysis of income and PESEL

A frequent problem with family and local benefits.

4. Lack of proof of employment

It is worth collecting the contract, ZUS registrations, payslips and confirmations of contributions.

5. The same approach for everyone

For a third-country national you must check an additional residence condition.

6. Underestimating the stage of the case

We assess a client "on the stamp" differently from one with an issued residence card.

FAQ

Questions and answers

Is a foreigner on a mandate contract entitled to sickness allowance?
Yes, but only if they opted into voluntary sickness insurance and the 90-day qualifying period has elapsed. With an employment contract, sickness insurance is compulsory and the qualifying period is 30 days.
Will I get 800+ for my child if I have temporary residence and work?
The key factor is the "access to the labour market" annotation on the residence card and living together with the child in Poland. The stamp after filing the application alone is often insufficient — each situation must be assessed individually.
After how long of working can I get unemployment benefit?
As a rule, after proving 365 days of work with Labour Fund contributions paid within the 18 months before registration at the labour office — and provided you hold an appropriate residence title.
Does the right to benefits depend on citizenship?
Not directly. It is decided by a combination: residence title, right to work, ZUS insurance coverage and — for some benefits — the income criterion.
Legal basis

Legal basis and sources

Legal acts (as of July 2026)

  • Act of 25 June 1999 on cash benefits from social insurance in the event of sickness and maternity (the so-called benefits act).
  • Act of 13 October 1998 on the social insurance system.
  • Act of 30 October 2002 on social insurance for accidents at work and occupational diseases.
  • Act of 11 February 2016 on state aid in raising children (the 800+ benefit).
  • Act of 28 November 2003 on family benefits.
  • Act of 20 March 2025 on the labour market and employment services (in force from 1 June 2025; replaced the act on employment promotion).
  • Act of 12 March 2004 on social assistance (art. 5 — catalogue of eligible foreigners).
  • Act of 12 December 2013 on foreigners (the residence bases referred to by the above acts).
