Watch out

How not to get cheated

The golden rule: you do not pay an intermediary merely for finding you a job in Poland. If someone demands a fee "for arranging work" or offers wages "off the books" without a contract — that is a warning sign.

What to look out for

Check the employment agency in the official National Register of Employment Agencies (KRAZ) — a legal agency must be entered there. Always demand a written contract in a language you understand, and never hand your passport over to anyone "for safekeeping". Beware of housing offers where you pay in advance before seeing the property.

If your employer breaks the law (does not pay, does not register you with ZUS), free help is offered by the National Labour Inspectorate — including under the "Foreigners at work" campaign. You will find the links in the sources at the bottom.

Frequently asked questions

FAQ

Is the guide only for workers from Ukraine?
No. The advice applies to every foreigner coming to Poland for work — regardless of country of origin. Some information (e.g. about bus connections) is particularly useful for people from the East.
Do I need a PESEL to start work?
Not always to start work itself, but a PESEL makes life much easier — it is needed for an employment/mandate contract, a bank account and tax settlements, among other things. Details in the "First steps" section.
How do I know whether an employment agency is legal?
Check its entry in the National Register of Employment Agencies (KRAZ) — a free online search engine run by the Ministry. The absence of an entry is a serious warning sign.
